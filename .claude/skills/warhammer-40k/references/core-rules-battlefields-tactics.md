# Core Rules — Battlefields and Tactics (Sections 13–16, pp.44–59)

Source: *Warhammer 40,000* Core Rules booklet, "Battlefields and Tactics"
chapter — 13. Terrain, 14. Objectives, 15. Stratagems, 16. Actions.

## Terrain (Section 13)

### 13.01 — Placing Terrain

Before the battle, place a selection of **terrain features** on the
battlefield using one or more of these methods:

- Place a well-defined boundary (e.g. a base or mat) in each location you
  want to furnish with terrain, then place one or more **terrain
  features** wholly within that boundary.
- Place one **terrain feature** directly on the battlefield.
- Place two or more **terrain features** directly on the battlefield in
  such a way that together they define the boundary of an area.

In each case, the area of the battlefield occupied by that boundary or
**terrain feature** is a **terrain area**. A mission's deployment map may
define the location and dimensions of each **terrain area**; otherwise
players must agree on this before the battle.

**Sidebar — Creating your own battlefield:** If your terrain doesn't
match a mission's **terrain category** requirements, just do your best
with the **terrain features** you have. If a mission gives no guidance,
players decide how to build the battlefield themselves. When doing so,
keep in mind that **dense terrain features** have the greatest impact on
visibility and movement — too few of them can advantage shooting-heavy
armies (or disadvantage melee-heavy ones). Leave enough room around
**dense terrain features** for larger models such as **MONSTERS**/
**VEHICLES** to manoeuvre, especially near the battlefield edges.

### 13.02 — Terrain Categories

Every **terrain feature** belongs to a **terrain category**, which can
affect the movement and visibility of models. A mission's deployment map
may define which **terrain categories** should be present within each
**terrain area** for the best game experience. **Terrain features**
sharing the same **terrain area** can belong to different **terrain
categories**.

#### Exposed (13.03)

Offers only scant protection to the most desperate troops; can be
traversed without hindrance.
**Examples:** craters, razorwire, scattered debris.

#### Light (13.04)

Can provide cover from incoming attacks, but will not slow an enemy's
advance or offer lasting defence.
**Examples:** barricades, low walls, statuary.

#### Dense (13.05)

An obstacle even to the largest war machines, and can shelter entire
squads from enemy sight.
**Examples:** buildings, ruins, armoured containers, woods.

### 13.06 — Terrain and Movement

Models can move through different categories of **terrain feature** as
follows:

- **Exposed/Light:** All models can move horizontally and vertically
  through **exposed** and **light terrain features**.
- **Dense:**
  - **INFANTRY/BEASTS/SWARM/MOBILE** models can move horizontally
    through **dense terrain features**.
  - **INFANTRY/BEASTS/SWARM** models can move vertically through
    **dense terrain features**.
  - Other models can move horizontally through **dense terrain
    features** provided that all sections of that **terrain feature**
    that the moving model's base would move through are 2" or less in
    height. Otherwise, the moving model must move vertically to ascend
    or descend such sections. They cannot move through ceilings and
    floors while doing so, and cannot end that move on any surface of
    that **terrain feature** that is not on ground level.

#### Moving Vertically

Models can move vertically to ascend or descend **terrain features**.
While doing so:

- That model must remain within ⅓" horizontally of that **terrain
  feature**. **Unresolved ambiguity**: the source page (Core Rules p.48)
  renders this fraction with a stylized glyph that two independent
  vision-model reads disagreed on — one read ⅓", a second independent
  read of the same page read ⅔". Don't treat either as settled; verify
  against a physical card/rulebook or the official app before relying on
  this number for anything precision-sensitive.
- Add the distance moved vertically up, and the distance moved vertically
  down, to any other distance that model has moved since its unit began
  that move.

**Sidebar — Plunging Fire:** As well as gaining superior lines of sight,
models that ascend tall **terrain features** can benefit from the
Plunging Fire rule (22.05, outside this section's page range).

#### Setting Up or Ending a Move

Models can be set up or end a move on the **ground level** of **terrain
features**. Models can also be set up or end a move on any surface of a
**terrain feature** that is not on ground level, if **all** of the
following apply:

- That model has one or more of the following keywords:
  **INFANTRY/BEASTS/SWARM/FLY/MONSTER**.
- After ending that move, that model is stable and no part of its base
  overhangs the outer edge of that surface.

**Sidebar — Solid Terrain:** If a **terrain feature** has the **Solid**
rule (13.11), as **dense terrain features** do, a model cannot end a move
with any part of its base through any enclosed part of that **terrain
feature** that is 3" or less from ground level — not even through small
openings such as doors and windows. This ensures protruding elements of
models cannot be used to circumvent the visibility restrictions of the
**Solid** rule.

### 13.07 — Terrain and Visibility

Terrain can affect visibility depending on whether the **Benefit of
Cover**, **Hidden**, **Obscuring**, or **Solid** rules apply.

#### Benefit of Cover (13.08)

Each time a ranged attack targets a unit, if **every** model in that unit
meets one or more of the following conditions, that unit has the
**benefit of cover** against that attack:

- That model has the **INFANTRY/BEASTS/SWARM** keyword and is within a
  **terrain area**.
- That model is not **fully visible** to the attacking model due to one
  or more intervening **terrain features** and/or one or more
  intervening **obscuring terrain areas** (see below).

Each time a ranged attack targets a unit that has the **benefit of
cover** against it, worsen the **BS** characteristic of that attack by 1.

#### Hidden (13.09)

A model is **hidden** while **all** of the following apply to it:

- That model has the **INFANTRY/BEASTS/SWARM** keyword and is within a
  **terrain area** that contains one or more **dense terrain features**.
- That model's unit did not make one or more ranged attacks during this
  turn or during the previous turn.

While a model is **hidden**, it can only be **visible** to enemy models
that are within its **detection range**. Unless otherwise stated, a
model's **detection range** is 15".

#### Obscuring (13.10)

**Terrain areas** containing one or more **light** or **dense terrain
features** are **obscuring terrain areas**. If every line of sight drawn
between two models crosses one or more **obscuring terrain areas**
(excluding **obscuring terrain areas** that one or both of those models
are within), those two models are not **visible** to each other.

#### Solid (13.11)

**Dense terrain features** have the **Solid** rule. Line of sight cannot
be drawn across any enclosed gap in the surface of such a **terrain
feature** that is 3" or less from ground level.

**Designer's Note:** This rule ensures that models are not **visible**
while sheltering in ground-level terrain, irrespective of small openings
such as doors, windows or bullet holes, or because of small gaps between
adjacent **terrain features**. 3" is the height of the first floor of
many **terrain features**, but some missions may adjust the height at
which this rule takes effect.

### Worked examples (illustrated on pp.47, 49, 51)

- **Terrain placed on a mat:** two ruin **terrain features** placed on a
  mat; the mat's edges define the boundary of the **terrain area**. Both
  features are ruins, so both use the **dense terrain feature** rules.
- **Terrain placed on the battlefield:** three **terrain features**
  placed directly on the battlefield; players agree the **terrain area**
  boundary the features define. Two are **dense**, one is **light** —
  features sharing a **terrain area** can differ in category.
- **Dense terrain and movement:** a **MONSTER** model cannot move through
  a ruin whose walls are all more than 2" tall, and must go around;
  **INFANTRY** models can move through the same walls freely.
- **Dense terrain, vertical movement:** an **INFANTRY** model can move
  vertically and end its move on the top surface of a **dense terrain
  feature** (stable, base not overhanging the edge). A **VEHICLE** cannot
  move through a **dense terrain feature**, but can move vertically to
  ascend/descend sections more than 2" tall (provided it doesn't end that
  move on top of such sections). An **INFANTRY** model moving through an
  open **terrain area** with no **terrain features** is unimpeded.
- **Benefit of cover:** comparing several units against a common firer —
  a unit fully visible with no **terrain area** membership has no
  **benefit of cover**; a unit only partly within a **terrain area** does
  not qualify (all models must meet a condition); a unit wholly within a
  **terrain area** does qualify; a unit not fully visible due to an
  **obscuring terrain area**, even without being in a **terrain area**
  itself, also qualifies. Each qualifying attack worsens the attacker's
  **BS** by 1 (shown as "-1 BS" on the diagram).
- **Hidden and obscuring:** an **obscuring terrain area** between two
  groups of units makes them not **visible** to each other. **INFANTRY**
  units within a **terrain area** containing **dense terrain** that
  haven't shot this or last turn are **hidden**, and are only **visible**
  to enemy models within 15" **detection range** — even though a
  **hidden** unit remains not visible to units outside that terrain area
  if no line of sight condition is met, it can still be seen by anyone
  within its detection range, regardless of whether they themselves are
  in a terrain area.
- **Solid:** two units separated by a window in a **dense terrain
  feature** are not **visible** to each other because the window is an
  enclosed gap 3" or less from ground level (the **Solid** rule blocks
  line of sight through it). Two units separated by an opening more than
  3" above ground level (e.g. a higher window) remain **visible** to each
  other, because **Solid** only blocks gaps at or below the 3" threshold.

## Objectives (Section 14)

Objectives represent critical locations both sides try to secure; many
missions require control of them to win.

### 14.01 — Terrain Objectives

If a mission uses **objectives**, it states where they are located,
typically via a deployment map showing points where **objectives** should
be placed. The location of each point should coincide with a **terrain
area** (13.01); that **terrain area** is the **objective**, called a
**terrain objective**.

When measuring distances to and from an **objective**, measure to and
from the closest part of it.

### 14.02 — Level of Control

At the start of the battle, no **objective** on the battlefield is
controlled by either player. To gain control of an **objective**, a
player needs one or more models with an **OC** characteristic of 1 or
more within range of it. A model is within range of a **terrain
objective** while it is within that **terrain area**.

At the end of each phase and turn, to determine a player's **level of
control** over an **objective**, add together the **OC** characteristics
of all models in that player's army that are within range of that
**objective**:

- The player with the highest **level of control** over that objective
  controls it.
- If both players have the same **level of control** over that objective,
  unless that objective is **secured** (see below), it is not controlled
  by either player.

While one or more units from a player's army are within range of an
**objective** that player controls, for each of those units that
contains one or more models with an **OC** characteristic of 1 or more,
that unit is said to be **controlling** that objective.

**Sidebar — Hold At All Costs:** Some units have abilities triggered when
they control an **objective**, or when they **secure** an **objective**
for your army.

### 14.03 — Secured Objectives

Some rules allow an **objective** to be **secured** by a player's army.
When an **objective** is **secured** by a player's army, that objective
remains under their control — even once they no longer have any units
within range of it — until their opponent's **level of control** over
that objective is greater than theirs at the end of a phase.

### Worked example: Controlling a Terrain Objective (p.53)

- A **VEHICLE** with **OC** 3 is within a **terrain area**; another
  **VEHICLE** with **OC** 2 is wholly within the same **terrain area**;
  a unit of models each with **OC** 1 has six of its models within the
  area. A separate unit is currently **battle-shocked**, so the **OC**
  characteristic of all of its models counts as "-" (i.e. contributes
  nothing).
- Both players add together the **OC** characteristics of all their
  models within the **terrain area**. In the example, the blue player's
  **level of control** is 5 and the red player's is 6 — the red player
  therefore controls the objective.

## Stratagems (Section 15)

**Stratagems** are used by spending **Command Points (CP)** to create
epic moments of tactical brilliance or peerless martial might, deployed
when a player judges a critical point in the battle has been reached.

### 15.01 — Using Stratagems

During the battle, both players can use **stratagems**. Each **stratagem**
states:

- How many **CP** it costs to use.
- **WHEN:** when it can be used.
- **TARGET:** which units it can target.
- **EFFECT:** the effects of using it.
- **RESTRICTIONS:** any additional restrictions that apply.

Each player can use the same **stratagem** multiple times during the
battle, but:

- Each player cannot use the same **stratagem** more than once in the
  same phase.
- Unless otherwise stated, each player cannot target the same unit with
  more than one **stratagem** in the same phase.

Each time you use a **stratagem**, resolve this sequence:

1. Select targets as described in that **stratagem**.
2. Reduce your **CP** total by the **CP** cost listed. If that stratagem
   contains a section with an additional CP cost (e.g. "+1CP"), you may
   only use that additional section if you increase the CP cost of the
   stratagem by the amount shown — if you don't have enough CP remaining,
   you cannot use that section (or the whole stratagem, if you can't
   cover its base cost).
3. Resolve the effects of that **stratagem**.

**Core stratagems** (below) are usable by all players. Additional
**stratagems** can be found in Codexes and other publications.

**Stratagems key:** each stratagem card is marked with an icon indicating
when in the battle round it can be used: **Used in either player's turn**,
**Used in your turn**, or **Used in your opponent's turn** (see each
entry's WHEN below for the precise timing).

### Core Stratagems

#### Command Re-Roll (15.02) — 1CP — *Either player's turn*

*A great commander can bend even the vagaries of fate and fortune to
their will, the better to ensure victory.*

- **WHEN:** Any phase, just after you make one of the following rolls for
  a friendly unit or model: Advance roll, Charge roll, Damage roll,
  Hazard roll, Hit roll, Save roll, Wound roll, or a roll to determine
  the number of attacks generated by a weapon.
- **TARGET:** That unit or model.
- **EFFECT:** You re-roll that roll. If you are rolling more than one
  dice together, select one of those dice to re-roll (excluding **charge
  rolls**, which you must re-roll in full).

#### Epic Challenge (15.03) — 1CP — *Either player's turn*

*The legends of the 41st millennium are replete with deadly duels between
mighty champions.*

- **WHEN:** Fight phase, just after a friendly **CHARACTER** unit is
  selected to fight.
- **TARGET:** That **CHARACTER** unit.
- **EFFECT:** Select one **CHARACTER** model in your unit. Until the end
  of the phase, that model's melee weapons have the **[PRECISION]**
  ability.

#### Insane Bravery (15.04) — 1CP — *Your turn*

*Indifferent to their own survival, these warriors hold their ground
against seemingly impossible odds.*

- **WHEN:** Battle-shock step of your Command phase, just before you make
  a **battle-shock roll** for a friendly unit.
- **TARGET:** That unit.
- **EFFECT:** That **battle-shock roll** is automatically successful.
- **RESTRICTIONS:** You cannot use this stratagem more than once per
  battle.

#### Explosives (15.05) — 1CP — *Your turn*

*Priming grenades or other explosives, these warriors draw back and hurl
death into the enemy's midst.*

- **WHEN:** Your Shooting phase.
- **TARGET:** One friendly **unengaged EXPLOSIVES/GRENADES** unit that is
  eligible to shoot and did not make an **advance move** this turn.
- **EFFECT:** Resolve the following sequence:
  1. Select one **EXPLOSIVES/GRENADES** model in your unit.
  2. Select one **unengaged** enemy unit within 8" of and **visible** to
     that model.
  3. Roll six D6: for each 4+, that enemy unit suffers 1 **mortal wound**
     (06.02).

#### Crushing Impact (15.06) — 1CP — *Your turn*

*In extremis, armoured vehicles and rampaging monsters can use their
colossal size as a weapon, ramming and crushing enemies beneath their
bulk, though doing so risks sustaining damage in return.*

- **WHEN:** Your Charge phase, just after a friendly **MONSTER/VEHICLE**
  unit ends a **charge move**.
- **TARGET:** That **MONSTER/VEHICLE** unit.
- **EFFECT:** Resolve the following sequence:
  1. Select one enemy unit **engaged** with your unit.
  2. Select one model in your unit that is **engaged** with that unit.
  3. Roll a number of D6 equal to that model's **T** characteristic: for
     each 1, your unit suffers 1 **mortal wound**; for each 5+, that
     enemy unit suffers 1 **mortal wound** (to a maximum of 6 **mortal
     wounds** per unit).

#### Rapid Ingress (15.07) — 1CP — *Opponent's turn*

*Be it cunning strategy, potent technology or supernatural ritual, there
are many means by which a commander may hasten their warriors' onset.*

- **WHEN:** End of your opponent's Movement phase.
- **TARGET:** One friendly unit that is in **strategic reserves**
  (excluding **AIRCRAFT**).
- **EFFECT:** Your unit makes an **ingress move** (20.04).
- **RESTRICTIONS:** You cannot use this stratagem during the first battle
  round.

#### Fire Overwatch (15.08) — 1CP — *Opponent's turn*

*A hail of fire can drive back advancing foes.*

- **WHEN:** End of your opponent's Movement phase.
- **TARGET:** One friendly **unengaged** unit (excluding **TITANIC**
  units).
- **EFFECT:** Your unit shoots using **snap shooting** (see below).

#### Snap Shooting (15.09)

*Referenced by Fire Overwatch and other rules; not itself a Core
Stratagem (no CP cost) — defines a restricted shooting type.*

- **ELIGIBLE IF:** As stated in the rule allowing this shooting type.
- **EFFECT:** Your unit shoots as described in Making Attacks (04).
- **WHILE SHOOTING:**
  - You can only target one **visible** enemy unit within 24" of your
    unit (and only if it is an eligible target).
  - Each attack only hits on an unmodified **hit roll** of 6
    (irrespective of the attacking weapon's **BS** characteristic or any
    modifiers).
  - You cannot re-roll **hit rolls**.
- **AFTER SHOOTING:** Until the end of the phase, your unit is not
  **eligible to start an action**.

#### Smokescreen (15.10) — 1CP — *Opponent's turn*

*Even the most skilled marksmen struggle to hit targets veiled by
billowing screens of smoke.*

- **WHEN:** Start of your opponent's Shooting phase.
- **TARGET:** One friendly **SMOKE** unit.
- **EFFECT:** Until the end of the phase, each time an attack targets
  either your **SMOKE** unit, or a target that is not **fully visible**
  to the attacking model because of one or more models in your **SMOKE**
  unit, the target has the **benefit of cover** (13.08) against that
  attack.

#### Heroic Intervention (15.11) — 1CP (+1CP optional) — *Opponent's turn*

*Voices raised in furious war cries, your warriors surge forth to meet
the enemy's onslaught head-on.*

- **WHEN:** End of your opponent's Charge phase.
- **TARGET:** One friendly **unengaged** unit within 12" of one or more
  enemy units. You can only select a **VEHICLE** unit if it is a
  **CHARACTER/WALKER** unit.
- **EFFECT:** Resolve a **charge** with your unit (11.02). While doing so,
  before making the **charge roll**, you must select one of the following
  modes:
  - **Leap to Defend** (base cost, no extra CP): When selecting **charge
    targets**, you can only select enemy units that made a **charge
    move** this phase and are within the **maximum distance**.
  - **Into the Fray** (+1CP):
    - When making the **charge roll**, if the result is greater than 6
      (after modifiers), change it to 6.
    - When selecting **charge targets**, you can select **any** enemy
      units that are within 6" of your unit and within the **maximum
      distance**.

#### Counteroffensive (15.12) — 2CP — *Opponent's turn*

*In close-quarters combat, the slightest hesitation can leave an opening
for a swift foe to exploit.*

- **WHEN:** Fight step of your opponent's Fight phase, just after an
  enemy unit has resolved its attacks.
- **TARGET:** One friendly unit that is **eligible to fight**.
- **EFFECT:** Until the end of the phase, your unit has the **Fights
  First** ability, and it must be the next unit you **select to fight**
  (12.04).

## Actions (Section 16)

Actions represent vital battlefield tasks (e.g. searching for
archeotech, completing a ritual) that units carry out over the course of
the battle.

**Sidebar — Where to find actions:** the **actions** players can use are
found in other publications, such as mission packs. All players can use
**core actions**; other actions may have restrictions such as army
faction.

### 16.01 — Performing Actions

Some rules allow units to perform **actions**. Each **action** states:

- **STARTS:** When it is started.
- **UNITS:** Which friendly units can perform it.
- **USE LIMIT:** How many times friendly units can start it.
- **COMPLETES:** When it completes.
- **EFFECT:** The effects of completing it.
- Any additional restrictions that may apply.

#### Starting an Action

A unit is **eligible to start an action** unless one or more of the
following apply to it:

- It is not on the battlefield.
- It is an **AIRCRAFT/FORTIFICATION** unit.
- It is **battle-shocked**.
- It has an **OC** characteristic of 0 or "-".
- It is **engaged** (unless it is a **TITANIC** unit).
- It made an **advance** or **fall-back move** this turn.
- It started another **action** this turn.

If a unit starts an **action**, until the end of the turn:

- It is not **eligible to shoot** (excluding **TITANIC** units).
- It is not **eligible to declare a charge**.

#### Completing an Action

If a unit performing an **action** makes a move (excluding **pile-in**
and **consolidation moves**) or leaves the battlefield, that unit does
**not** complete that **action**. Otherwise, when an **action** is
completed, its "Effect" section is triggered.

### Example Action: Deploy Device (illustrative only; cannot be used)

*Though it won't turn the tide of battle, deployment of this device will
be instrumental to the war effort.*

- **STARTS:** Your Shooting phase.
- **UNITS:** One **INFANTRY** unit that is within a **terrain area** that
  is not within your deployment zone.
- **USE LIMIT:** Once per turn.
- **COMPLETES:** End of the turn.
- **EFFECT:** Set up one marker within 1" of your unit and within that
  **terrain area**.

## Key defined terms

- **Terrain feature** — A single physical piece of scenery placed on the
  battlefield; belongs to a terrain category (Exposed/Light/Dense).
- **Terrain area** — The battlefield area defined by a boundary (base,
  mat, or the footprint of one or more terrain features); may contain
  features of different categories.
- **Terrain category** — Exposed, Light, or Dense; determines a terrain
  feature's effect on movement and visibility.
- **Exposed terrain** — Category offering no real hindrance or lasting
  cover (craters, razorwire, debris).
- **Light terrain** — Category providing cover but not slowing movement
  or blocking sight for long (barricades, low walls, statuary).
- **Dense terrain** — Category that blocks movement for most models and
  can conceal whole squads (buildings, ruins, containers, woods); has the
  Solid rule.
- **Ground level** — The base elevation of a terrain feature/battlefield,
  as opposed to elevated surfaces within/on a terrain feature.
- **Benefit of cover** — Worsens the BS characteristic of a ranged attack
  by 1 against a qualifying target unit.
- **Hidden** — State of an INFANTRY/BEASTS/SWARM model in a terrain area
  with dense terrain that hasn't shot this or last turn; visible only
  within its detection range to enemies.
- **Detection range** — Default 15"; the range within which a hidden
  model can still be seen.
- **Obscuring (terrain area)** — A terrain area containing light or dense
  terrain features; blocks visibility between two models whose line of
  sight crosses it (unless one/both models are within it).
- **Solid** — Rule on dense terrain features blocking line of sight
  through any enclosed gap 3" or less from ground level.
- **Fully visible** — A visibility state used to determine benefit of
  cover and obscuring; a model not "fully visible" to an attacker (due to
  intervening terrain/obscuring areas) contributes to cover.
- **Objective / terrain objective** — A critical battlefield location
  (coinciding with a terrain area) that armies compete to control.
- **OC (Objective Control)** — A model characteristic; summed per army
  within range of an objective to determine level of control.
- **Level of control** — The sum of a player's models' OC characteristics
  within range of an objective; the higher total controls it (ties
  control nobody, unless secured).
- **Controlling (an objective)** — State of a unit with one or more OC
  1+ models within range of an objective its player controls.
- **Secured (objective)** — An objective that stays under a player's
  control even after they leave its range, until the opponent's level of
  control exceeds theirs at the end of a phase.
- **Stratagem** — A CP-cost battlefield effect with WHEN/TARGET/EFFECT/
  RESTRICTIONS, usable once per phase per stratagem, per player.
- **CP (Command Points)** — Resource spent to use stratagems.
- **Core stratagem** — A stratagem usable by all players regardless of
  army/codex.
- **Snap shooting** — A restricted shooting type (hits only on unmodified
  6s, no hit re-rolls, single visible target within 24") granted by
  certain rules (e.g. Fire Overwatch); using it makes the unit ineligible
  to start an action for the rest of the phase.
- **Action** — A battlefield task with STARTS/UNITS/USE LIMIT/COMPLETES/
  EFFECT; starting one blocks shooting and charging for the rest of the
  turn (except TITANIC units for shooting); completes only if the
  performing unit doesn't move (beyond pile-in/consolidation) or leave
  the battlefield.
- **Eligible to start an action** — Excludes units off the battlefield,
  AIRCRAFT/FORTIFICATION, battle-shocked, OC 0/"-", engaged (unless
  TITANIC), that advanced/fell back this turn, or that already started an
  action this turn.
- **Ingress move** — The move made by a unit entering from strategic
  reserves (referenced via Rapid Ingress; full rule at 20.04, outside
  this range).
- **Strategic reserves** — Off-battlefield holding state for units that
  enter later via an ingress move.
- **Fights First** — Ability granted to a unit (e.g. by Counteroffensive)
  making it fight before other units in the Fight phase and forcing it to
  be selected next.
