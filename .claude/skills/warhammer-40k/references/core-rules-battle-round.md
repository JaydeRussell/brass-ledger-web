# Core Rules — The Battle Round (pp.26–43)

Source: Warhammer 40,000 Core Rules booklet, section 07–12 ("The Battle
Round"). Numbers in parentheses like `(09.05)` are the rulebook's own
subsection numbers, reproduced here so they can be cited precisely.
Cross-references to sections outside pages 26–43 (e.g. `Actions 16.00`,
`Terrain and Visibility 13.07`, `Strategic Reserves 20.00`) are noted as
pointers only — their content lives in other parts of the rulebook, not
in this file.

## The Battle Round (07)

Games of Warhammer 40,000 are played in a series of **battle rounds**.
Each battle round is resolved by following these steps:

1. **Start of Battle Round**
2. **Player Turns**
3. **End of Battle Round**

### 1. Start of Battle Round (07.01)

Rules that are triggered at the start of the battle round are resolved
now, before progressing to player turns.

### 2. Player Turns (07.02)

Both players now take one turn each. The same player always takes the
first turn in each battle round — the mission being played determines
which player this is. Once that player's turn has ended, their opponent
takes their turn.

Each **turn** consists of seven parts: first the **Start of Turn** step,
then a series of five phases resolved in this order, then the **End of
Turn** step:

1. Start of Turn step
2. **Command phase** — marshal strategic resources and check units' morale
3. **Movement phase** — units move across the battlefield; strategic
   reserves arrive
4. **Shooting phase** — units make attacks with ranged weapons
5. **Charge phase** — units make charge moves to engage the enemy
6. **Fight phase** — both players' units make attacks with melee weapons
7. End of Turn step

### 3. End of Battle Round (07.03)

Rules triggered at the end of the battle round are resolved now, in this
order:

1. First resolve rules triggered at this point other than mission rules.
2. Both players then consult their mission; if one or both players have
   achieved any aspects of their mission that are triggered at this
   point, resolve them now.

The battle round then ends and, unless the battle ends, the next battle
round starts. The mission being played determines how many battle
rounds are resolved before the battle ends.

### End of Turn step (part of the seven-part turn)

Rules triggered at the end of a turn are resolved now, in the following
order:

1. First resolve rules triggered at this point other than mission rules.
2. Both players then consult their mission; if one or both players have
   achieved any aspects of their mission that are triggered at this
   point, resolve them now.

## Command Phase (08)

In the Command phase, both players gain **Core CP**, then you check the
combat readiness of your forces and use any strategic-level abilities
you may have. Resolved by following these steps:

1. Start of Command phase
2. Gain Core CP
3. Battle-shock
4. Command Abilities
5. End of Command phase

### 1. Start of Command Phase (08.01)

Rules triggered at the start of the Command phase are resolved now.

### 2. Gain Core CP (08.02)

Both players gain **1 Command Point (CP)**.

> **Command Points** are a valuable resource you can spend to use
> **stratagems** (section 15). The Gain Core CP step ensures both
> players gain 1 CP each turn. While these are termed **Core CP** here,
> they are Command Points like any other — other rules sometimes refer
> to "Core CP" specifically when referencing these Command Points.

### 3. Battle-shock (08.03)

The active player must make one **battle-shock roll** (cross-reference:
01.07, outside this section's page range) for each unit in their army
that fulfils one or both of the following conditions:

- That unit is currently **battle-shocked**.
- That unit is at, or below, **half-strength**.

If a unit was battle-shocked at the start of this step and its
battle-shock roll during this step succeeds, it is no longer
battle-shocked.

**Worked examples (battle-shock, p.31):**

| Unit | Starting strength | Current state | Half-strength? | Battle-shocked? | Roll required? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Infantry unit A | 3 | Not reduced | No | Currently battle-shocked | Yes — battle-shocked alone triggers it |
| Infantry unit B | 10 | 5 models remaining | At half-strength | — | Yes |
| Infantry unit C | 5 | 2 models remaining | Below half-strength | — | Yes |
| Vehicle unit (W 11) | 1 (a Vehicle's starting strength is 1) | 3 wounds remaining | Below half-strength (wounds vs. W characteristic) | — | Yes |

Cross-references named but not detailed in this page range: Modified
Characteristics, Multiple Battle-shock Rolls, Not on the Battlefield,
Starting Strength and Half-strength.

### 4. Command Abilities (08.04)

Rules that are triggered in the Command phase (excluding those
triggered at the start or end of this phase, by gaining Core CP, or by
battle-shock rolls) are resolved now.

### 5. End of Command Phase (08.05)

Rules triggered at the end of the Command phase are resolved now, in
this order:

1. First resolve rules triggered at this point other than mission rules.
2. Both players then consult their mission; if one or both players have
   achieved any aspects of their mission triggered at this point,
   resolve them now.

## Movement Phase (09)

In the Movement phase, you move each of your units across the
battlefield. Reinforcements can also arrive in this phase to bolster
your forces. Resolved by following these steps:

1. Start of Movement phase
2. Move Units
3. End of Movement phase

### 1. Start of Movement Phase (09.01)

Rules triggered at the start of the Movement phase are resolved now.

### 2. Move Units (09.02)

The active player moves their units one at a time, using this sequence,
until all of their units have been **selected to move** and those moves
have ended:

1. **Select Unit:** Select one friendly unit that has not been selected
   to move this phase. You can select a unit on the battlefield, in
   **strategic reserves**, or embarked within a **TRANSPORT**. That unit
   is selected to move.
2. **Select Move Type:** Select one **move type** that unit is eligible
   to make, and resolve it with that unit. This can be one of the types
   below, or one presented elsewhere:
   - Remain stationary (09.04)
   - Normal move (09.05)
   - Advance move (09.06)
   - Fall-back move (09.07)
   - Disembark move (cross-ref 18.04, Transports — outside this range)
   - Ingress move (cross-ref 20.04, Strategic Reserves — outside this range)

> **Selecting units to move:** When the Move Units step ends, the
> active player must have selected all of their units to make a move,
> including those in strategic reserves. This means that before the
> phase ends, every unit in their army will have been selected to make
> a move, even if it is to **remain stationary**.

### 3. End of Movement Phase (09.03)

Rules triggered at the end of the Movement phase are resolved now.

### Move type reference table

#### Remain Stationary (09.04)

- **Maximum distance:** "–" (no movement)
- **Eligible if:** Any unit.
- **Effect:** No models are moved (either in straight lines or
  rotated). Units that remain stationary do **not** trigger any rules
  that are triggered when a unit starts or ends a move.

#### Normal Move (09.05)

- **Maximum distance:** the unit's **M** characteristic.
- **Eligible if:** Unit is on the battlefield and **unengaged**.
- **Effect:** Unit moves as described in Moving (section 03, outside
  this range).
- **After moving:** Unit must be unengaged.

#### Advance Move (09.06)

- **Maximum distance:** Advance roll + the unit's **M** characteristic.
- **Eligible if:** Unit is on the battlefield and unengaged.
- **Effect:** Unit moves as described in Moving (03).
- **Before moving:** Make an **advance roll** by rolling one D6.
- **After moving:**
  - Unit must be unengaged.
  - Until the end of the turn, unless otherwise stated, the unit is
    **not eligible to declare a charge or start an action**.

#### Fall-Back Move (09.07)

- **Maximum distance:** the unit's **M** characteristic.
- **Eligible if:** Unit is **engaged**.
- **Effect:** Unit moves as described in Moving (03).
- **Before moving:** Select a **fall-back mode**:
  - **Ordered Retreat:** If your unit is not battle-shocked, you can
    select this mode.
  - **Desperate Escape:** Otherwise, you must select this mode. Make a
    **hazard roll** for each model in your unit (cross-ref 06.03,
    outside this range).
- **While moving:**
  - Desperate Escape: Each model that is moved can be moved through
    enemy models.
- **After moving:**
  - Unit must be unengaged.
  - Until the end of the turn, unless otherwise stated, the unit is not
    eligible to shoot, declare a charge, or start an action.
  - Desperate Escape: If your unit is not battle-shocked, you must make
    a battle-shock roll for your unit (01.07).

> **Selecting modes:** Some rules instruct you to select a mode, such as
> fall-back modes. Modes are mutually exclusive, and you must assess
> each one in the order presented. When making a move, if your unit
> does not meet the conditions of any of the modes, it cannot make that
> move.
>
> Sometimes a mode is mandatory if applicable (e.g. **consolidation
> modes**, 12.08), but in the case of fall-back moves, Ordered Retreat
> is not mandatory, so you could select Desperate Escape instead.
>
> Many move types state conditions you must meet while/after moving.
> Those labelled with a mode name only apply if you selected that mode;
> those not labelled with a mode name always apply.

## Shooting Phase (10)

In the Shooting phase, your units take aim and use their ranged weapons
to fire at their chosen targets. Resolved by following these steps:

1. Start of Shooting phase
2. Shoot
3. End of Shooting phase

### 1. Start of Shooting Phase (10.01)

Rules triggered at the start of the Shooting phase are resolved now.

### 2. Shoot (10.02)

The active player shoots with their **eligible** units one at a time,
using this sequence, until all the units they choose to shoot with have
been **selected to shoot** and their attacks have been resolved.

A unit is **eligible to shoot** if it is on the battlefield and has not
already been selected to shoot this phase.

1. **Select Unit:** Select one friendly unit that is eligible to shoot;
   that unit is selected to shoot.
2. **Select Shooting Type:** Select one **shooting type** that unit is
   eligible to make, and resolve it with that unit. This can be one
   listed below, or one presented elsewhere:
   - Normal shooting (10.04)
   - Assault shooting (10.05)
   - Close-quarters shooting (10.06)
   - Indirect shooting (10.07)

### 3. End of Shooting Phase (10.03)

Rules triggered at the end of the Shooting phase are resolved now.

### Shooting type reference table

#### Normal Shooting (10.04)

- **Eligible if:** Your unit is unengaged and did not make an advance
  move this turn.
- **Effect:** Your unit shoots as described in Making Attacks (section
  04, outside this range).
- **After shooting:** Until the end of the phase, your unit is not
  eligible to start an action.

#### Assault Shooting (10.05)

- **Eligible if:** All of the following apply to your unit:
  - Unengaged and made an advance move this turn.
  - Has one or more **[ASSAULT]** weapons.
- **Effect:** Your unit shoots as described in Making Attacks (04).
- **While shooting:** You can only select **[ASSAULT]** weapons to make
  attacks.
- **After shooting:** Until the end of the phase, your unit is not
  eligible to start an action.

#### Close-Quarters Shooting (10.06)

- **Eligible if:** All of the following apply to your unit:
  - Engaged and did not make an advance move this turn.
  - Has one or more **[CLOSE-QUARTERS]** weapons, or is a
    **MONSTER**/**VEHICLE** unit.
- **Effect:** Your unit shoots as described in Making Attacks (04).
- **While shooting:** Models in your unit can target enemy units your
  unit is engaged with.
  - **MONSTER/VEHICLE models:** Each time such a model makes an attack:
    - Unless that attack is made with a [CLOSE-QUARTERS] weapon and
      targets a unit it is engaged with, subtract 1 from the **hit
      roll**.
    - If that attack is made with a **[BLAST]** weapon, it still cannot
      target a unit your unit is engaged with.
  - **Non-MONSTER/non-VEHICLE models:** You can only select
    [CLOSE-QUARTERS] weapons to make attacks, and you can only select
    enemy units that are engaged with your unit as targets.
- **After shooting:** Until the end of the phase, your unit is not
  eligible to start an action.

#### Indirect Shooting (10.07)

- **Eligible if:** All of the following apply to your unit:
  - Unengaged and did not make an advance move this turn.
  - Has one or more **[INDIRECT FIRE]** weapons.
- **Effect:** Your unit shoots as described in Making Attacks (04).
- **While shooting:**
  - [INDIRECT FIRE] weapons in your unit can target enemy units that
    are not **visible** to the attacking model.
  - Each time an [INDIRECT FIRE] weapon makes an attack against a
    target that is not visible:
    - The target has the **benefit of cover** against that attack
      (cross-ref 13.08, Terrain and Visibility — outside this range).
    - You cannot re-roll hit rolls.
    - An unmodified hit roll of 1–5 fails, unless the target
      **remained stationary** this turn and the target is visible to
      one or more friendly units, in which case an unmodified hit roll
      of 1–3 fails instead.
- **After shooting:** Until the end of the phase, your unit is not
  eligible to start an action.

> **Indirect fire:** When you select indirect shooting for a unit, its
> [INDIRECT FIRE] weapons can launch punishing barrages on targets that
> are not visible, but don't forget that its other weapons can still
> target other visible targets.

## Charge Phase (11)

In the Charge phase, you make charge moves with your units,
representing your forces closing the distance with their foes to engage
them in close combat. Resolved by following these steps:

1. Start of Charge phase
2. Charge
3. End of Charge phase

### 1. Start of Charge Phase (11.01)

Rules triggered at the start of the Charge phase are resolved now.

### 2. Charge (11.02)

The active player resolves **charges** with their eligible units one at
a time, using this sequence, until all of their units that they choose
to charge with have **declared a charge** and those charges have been
resolved.

1. **Declare Charge:** Select one friendly unit that has not declared a
   charge this phase and is **eligible to declare a charge** (see
   below). That unit declares a charge.

   A unit is eligible to declare a charge if it is on the battlefield,
   unless otherwise stated. Rules that **prevent** a unit from being
   eligible to declare a charge:
   - It is not within 12" of one or more enemy units.
   - It is **engaged**.
   - It made an advance or fall-back move this turn.
2. **Make Charge Roll:** Make a **charge roll** by rolling 2D6; the
   result is the maximum distance for the charge move.
3. **Attempt Charge:** If it is possible to make a **charge move** with
   that unit, and you still want to, make a charge move (see below).
   Otherwise, your unit does not make a charge move. In either case,
   the charge is then resolved.

> **Failed charges:** In the absence of modifiers to the charge roll, a
> result of 2 (double 1) is never sufficient for a unit to complete a
> charge move, as a unit cannot be within **engagement range** (2")
> when it attempts a charge. Such a roll would therefore result in a
> failed charge, and the unit would not move.

### 3. End of Charge Phase (11.03)

Rules triggered at the end of the Charge phase are resolved now.

### Charge Move (11.04)

- **Maximum distance:** the charge roll (2D6 result).
- **Eligible if:** Your unit declared a charge this phase.
- **Effect:** Your unit moves as described in Moving (03).
- **Before moving:** Select one or more enemy units that are within 12"
  of your unit and within the maximum distance of your unit; until the
  end of this move, each is a **charge target**.
- **While moving:**
  - Each model must end its move closer to one or more charge targets.
  - Each model that can end its move within 1" of one or more charge
    targets must do so.
  - Each model that can end its move engaged with one or more charge
    targets must do so.
- **After moving:**
  - Your unit must be engaged with all of the charge targets.
  - Your unit cannot be engaged with one or more enemy units that are
    not charge targets.
  - Until the end of the turn, each model in your unit has the **Fights
    First** ability (cross-ref 24.13, outside this range — grants
    priority to fight before other units in the Fight phase).

**Worked example ("Making a Charge Move," p.37):** A charging unit
declares a charge and rolls a charge roll generating a maximum distance
of 7". An enemy unit within that 7" can be selected as a charge target
even if the charging unit's move would not end engaged with it (after
manoeuvring around intervening terrain) — selection only requires being
within the maximum distance, not guaranteed engagement. A unit more
than 7" away cannot be selected as a charge target at all, even if the
charging unit's charge move could end within 2" of it. Two units both
within 7" can both be selected as charge targets if the charging unit
can end its charge move engaged with all of them.

## Fight Phase (12)

In the Fight phase, both players get to act. First, units pile in to
maximise the number of models that are engaged, then all the combatants
make melee attacks, before units consolidate their position. Resolved
by following these steps:

1. Start of Fight phase
2. Pile In
3. Fight
4. Consolidate
5. End of Fight phase

### 1. Start of Fight Phase (12.01)

Rules triggered at the start of the Fight phase are resolved now.

> **Worked example (p.39):** If both of a player's units made charge
> moves this turn, both have the Fights First ability this phase and
> are both eligible to make pile-in moves — even if one of them (e.g. a
> MONSTER) is currently unengaged because it destroyed its charge
> target during the Charge phase (in the example, via the "Crushing
> Impact" stratagem, 15.06).

### 2. Pile In (12.02)

Both players make **pile-in moves** with all of their eligible units
they choose to move. The "Eligible If" section of the Pile-in Move
rules describes which units are eligible to make such moves. The player
whose turn it is resolves all of their moves first, followed by their
opponent. Each unit cannot make more than one pile-in move during this
step.

#### Pile-In Move (12.03)

- **Maximum distance:** 3"
- **Eligible if:** It is the Fight phase and one or more of the
  following apply to your unit:
  - It is engaged.
  - It made a charge move this turn.
  - It was selected to make an **overrun fight** this phase (12.06).
- **Effect:** Your unit moves as described in Moving (03).
- **Before moving:** Select **pile-in targets**:
  - If your unit is engaged, select every enemy unit it is engaged
    with.
  - Otherwise, select one or more enemy units within 5" of your unit.
- **While moving:**
  - Models in base-contact with one or more enemy models cannot be
    moved.
  - Each model that is moved must end its move closer to the closest
    pile-in target, and engaged with it if possible.
- **After moving:**
  - Your unit must be engaged.
  - Each model that started this move engaged with an enemy unit must
    still be engaged with that enemy unit.

> **Do units have to fight?** Yes, you have to fight with all units
> that can, but you don't have to pile in or consolidate with a unit if
> you don't want to.

**Worked example (pile-in moves, p.39):** The player whose units made
pile-in moves resolves them first; the opponent's units make pile-in
moves next, but if their models are already in base-contact with enemy
models, they are not moved.

### 3. Fight (12.04)

A unit is **eligible to fight** if it has not already been **selected
to fight** this phase and one or more of the following apply to it:

- It is engaged, or it was engaged at the start of this step.
- It made a charge move this turn.

Players resolve the following sequence until all eligible units have
been selected to fight and their attacks have been resolved:

1. **Resolve Fights First Combats:** Starting with the player whose
   turn it is, players alternate selecting one friendly **Fights
   First** unit that is eligible to fight; that unit is selected to
   fight. If this is not possible:
   - If there are no Fights First units that are eligible to fight,
     move to the Resolve Remaining Combats step.
   - Otherwise, the other player selects their next unit.
2. **Resolve Remaining Combats:** Starting with the player who just
   moved this sequence onto this step, players alternate selecting one
   friendly unit that is eligible to fight; that unit is selected to
   fight. If this is not possible:
   - If there are no units that are eligible to fight, the Fight step
     ends.
   - Otherwise, the other player selects their next unit.

After resolving a fight in the Resolve Remaining Combats step, if one
or more Fights First units are now eligible to fight, return to the
Resolve Fights First Combats step.

**When a unit is selected to fight:** select one **fight type** that it
is eligible to make, and resolve it. This can be one listed below, or
one presented elsewhere:

#### Normal Fight (12.05)

- **Eligible if:** Your unit is engaged.
- **Effect:** Your unit fights as described in Making Attacks (04).

#### Overrun Fight (12.06)

- **Eligible if:** Your unit is unengaged, or was unengaged at the
  start of the Fight step but became engaged during the Fight phase.
- **Effect:** Your unit can make one additional pile-in move, then
  fights as described in Making Attacks (04).

> **Overrun fights:** When a unit makes an overrun fight, its models
> can be moved such that enemy units that were unengaged become
> engaged. Such enemy units become eligible to fight this phase (and
> may even be able to fight next, if they are Fights First units).

**Worked examples (pp.40–41):**

- **Normal Fight:** A player selects their MONSTER to fight; it makes a
  normal fight, destroying an enemy TRANSPORT. The unit that was
  embarked inside makes an **emergency disembark move**; after doing
  so, it is unengaged.
- **Overrun Fight:** The opposing player has no Fights First units
  eligible, so the first player selects their next unit — another
  Fights First unit. This unit is unengaged (it was engaged with the
  destroyed TRANSPORT at the start of the Fight step), so it can make
  an overrun fight: it first makes a pile-in move to engage the unit
  that just disembarked, then fights, destroying two enemy models. The
  disembarked unit then fights back but inflicts no damage.

### 4. Consolidate (12.07)

Both players make **consolidation moves** with all of their eligible
units they choose to move. The "Eligible If" section describes which
units are eligible to make such moves. The player whose turn it is
resolves all of their moves first, followed by their opponent. Each
unit cannot make more than one consolidation move during this step.

#### Consolidation Move (12.08)

- **Maximum distance:** 3"
- **Eligible if:** It is the Fight phase and your unit was **eligible
  to fight** this phase.
- **Effect:** Your unit moves as described in Moving (03).
- **Before moving:** Select a **consolidation mode**:
  - **Ongoing Consolidation:** If your unit is engaged, you must select
    this mode and select every enemy unit it is engaged with.
  - **Engaging Consolidation:** Otherwise, if your unit is within 3" of
    one or more enemy units, you must select this mode and select one
    or more of those enemy units.
  - **Objective Consolidation:** Otherwise, if your unit is within 3"
    of one or more **objectives**, you must select this mode and
    select one of those objectives.
- **While moving:**
  - Ongoing Consolidation: Models in base-contact with one or more
    enemy models cannot be moved. Each model that is moved must end
    its move closer to the closest selected enemy unit, and engaged
    with it if possible.
  - Engaging Consolidation: Each model that is moved must end its move
    closer to the closest selected enemy unit, and engaged with it if
    possible.
  - Objective Consolidation: Each model that is moved must end its
    move within range of the selected objective if possible, or closer
    to it if not.
- **After moving:**
  - Ongoing Consolidation: Each model that started this move engaged
    with an enemy unit must still be engaged with that enemy unit.
  - Engaging Consolidation: Your unit must be engaged with all of the
    selected enemy units. If one or more units you have selected have
    not been selected to fight this phase, your opponent must select
    each of those units, one at a time; when each is selected, it
    becomes eligible to fight and is selected to fight (12.04).
  - Objective Consolidation: Your unit must be within range of the
    selected objective.

> **New foes to face:** While using the Engaging Consolidation mode,
> your unit can end its consolidation move engaged with enemy units
> that have not yet fought this phase. If so, each of those enemy units
> will get an opportunity to fight your unit, so think carefully about
> how aggressively you want to move your unit using this mode.

**Worked examples (p.43):**

- **Ongoing Consolidation:** All engaged units, and all units that were
  eligible to fight this phase, can make a consolidation move; each
  model moves up to 3", but models in base-contact with enemy models
  cannot be moved.
- **Objective Consolidation:** No enemy units are within 3" of a
  MONSTER unit, but an objective is within 3", so the unit moves within
  range of that objective.

### 5. End of Fight Phase (12.09)

Rules triggered at the end of the Fight phase are resolved now.

## Key defined terms

- **Battle round** — one full cycle of both players' turns; a mission
  specifies how many are played before the battle ends.
- **Start of Battle Round** — step resolving rules that trigger before
  either player's turn.
- **Player turn** — one player's Start of Turn step, five phases, and
  End of Turn step; the same player always goes first each round.
- **End of Battle Round** — step resolving end-of-round triggers, then
  mission-triggered effects, after both players' turns.
- **Command phase** — phase 1 of a turn: gain Core CP, resolve
  battle-shock, use Command Abilities.
- **Movement phase** — phase 2: move units, including arrivals from
  strategic reserves.
- **Shooting phase** — phase 3: make ranged attacks.
- **Charge phase** — phase 4: make charge moves toward the enemy.
- **Fight phase** — phase 5: pile in, make melee attacks, consolidate.
- **Core CP** — the Command Points both players automatically gain (1
  each) every Command phase; functionally identical to CP from any
  other source.
- **Command Point (CP)** — a resource spent to use stratagems.
- **Stratagem** — an ability paid for with CP (defined fully in section
  15, outside this range).
- **Battle-shock roll** — a test made for a battle-shocked or
  half-strength (or below) unit during the Command phase; success
  removes the battle-shocked condition (full mechanic in 01.07, outside
  this range).
- **Battle-shocked** — a condition impairing a unit (its full effects
  are defined outside this page range) that persists until a successful
  battle-shock roll is made for it.
- **Half-strength** — a unit's current models/wounds at or below half
  of its starting strength; triggers a mandatory battle-shock roll.
- **Starting strength** — a unit's baseline model count (or, for a
  Vehicle, 1) used to judge half-strength.
- **Command Abilities** — Command-phase rules other than gaining Core
  CP or battle-shock rolls, resolved in step 4 of that phase.
- **Selected to move** — a unit that has been chosen to resolve a move
  this Movement phase; every unit must be selected to move (even if
  remaining stationary) before the phase ends.
- **Move type** — the category of move (Remain stationary, Normal,
  Advance, Fall-back, Disembark, Ingress, or others) chosen when a unit
  is selected to move.
- **Remain stationary** — a move type: no models moved; doesn't trigger
  start-/end-of-move rules.
- **Normal move** — a move type: move up to M, unit must be unengaged
  before and after.
- **Advance move** — a move type: move up to M + an advance roll (D6);
  forfeits eligibility to declare a charge or start an action this turn
  (unless stated otherwise).
- **Advance roll** — the D6 rolled before an advance move, added to M.
- **Fall-back move** — a move type used to disengage from combat;
  requires selecting Ordered Retreat or Desperate Escape.
- **Ordered Retreat** — a fall-back mode selectable only if the unit is
  not battle-shocked.
- **Desperate Escape** — the fall-back mode used when battle-shocked (or
  chosen voluntarily); requires a hazard roll per model, allows moving
  through enemy models, and forces a battle-shock roll afterward if the
  unit wasn't already battle-shocked.
- **Hazard roll** — a risk roll made per model during Desperate Escape
  (full mechanic in 06.03, outside this range).
- **Mode** — a set of mutually exclusive options a rule instructs you to
  choose between (e.g. fall-back modes, consolidation modes), assessed
  in the order presented; some modes are mandatory when applicable.
- **Unengaged / Engaged** — a unit's state relative to being within
  engagement range (2") of the enemy; many move/shoot/fight rules key
  off this state.
- **Selected to shoot** — a unit chosen to resolve a shooting type this
  Shooting phase; a unit can only be selected once per phase.
- **Eligible to shoot** — on the battlefield and not yet selected to
  shoot this phase.
- **Shooting type** — the category of shooting attack (Normal, Assault,
  Close-quarters, Indirect, or others) chosen when a unit is selected to
  shoot.
- **Normal shooting** — shooting type for an unengaged unit that didn't
  advance.
- **Assault shooting** — shooting type for a unit that advanced and has
  [ASSAULT] weapons; only those weapons may be used.
- **Close-quarters shooting** — shooting type for an engaged unit with
  [CLOSE-QUARTERS] weapons (or any MONSTER/VEHICLE), targeting units
  it's engaged with; non-[CLOSE-QUARTERS] attacks from MONSTER/VEHICLE
  models take a −1 to hit, and [BLAST] weapons still can't target
  engaged units.
- **Indirect shooting** — shooting type for an unengaged, non-advancing
  unit with [INDIRECT FIRE] weapons, letting those weapons target
  non-visible units (with cover, no re-rolling hits, and a to-hit
  penalty unless the shooter stayed stationary and is seen by a friendly
  unit).
- **[ASSAULT]** — weapon keyword allowing it to fire after an advance
  move (only usable via Assault shooting).
- **[CLOSE-QUARTERS]** — weapon keyword allowing it to fire at engaged
  enemy units (only usable via Close-quarters shooting).
- **[INDIRECT FIRE]** — weapon keyword allowing attacks against
  non-visible targets (only usable via Indirect shooting).
- **[BLAST]** — weapon keyword; cannot target a unit its firer is
  engaged with, even under Close-quarters shooting.
- **Visible** — line-of-sight condition required for normal targeting;
  Indirect Fire is the exception (full rules in 13.07, outside this
  range).
- **Benefit of cover** — a defensive bonus a target receives against
  certain attacks, including all Indirect Fire attacks against
  non-visible targets (full rules in 13.08, outside this range).
- **MONSTER / VEHICLE** — unit categories with special Close-quarters
  shooting interactions (defined fully in section 17, outside this
  range).
- **Declared a charge / Eligible to declare a charge** — a unit chosen
  to attempt a charge this Charge phase; ineligible if not within 12" of
  an enemy, if engaged, or if it advanced/fell back this turn.
- **Charge roll** — 2D6 rolled after declaring a charge; its result is
  the maximum distance for the charge move (a result of 2 always fails).
- **Charge target** — an enemy unit, within 12" and within the charge
  roll's distance, selected before a charge move; the charging unit must
  end engaged with all of them and not engaged with any non-targets.
- **Charge move** — the move type made after a successful charge
  attempt, up to the charge roll's distance.
- **Fights First** — an ability granted (until end of turn) to every
  model in a unit that completed a charge move, giving it priority
  selection in the Fight phase's Resolve Fights First Combats step
  (full definition in 24.13, outside this range).
- **Pile-in move** — a Fight-phase move (up to 3") that closes engaged
  or charging units on their pile-in targets, made in the Pile In step.
- **Pile-in target** — the enemy unit(s) a unit moves toward/engages
  during a pile-in move: every unit it's already engaged with, or one or
  more enemy units within 5" if not yet engaged.
- **Eligible to fight** — not yet selected to fight this phase, and
  either (still) engaged or made a charge move this turn.
- **Selected to fight** — a unit chosen to resolve a fight type in the
  Fight step; alternates between players, Fights First units first.
- **Fight type** — the category of melee resolution (Normal Fight,
  Overrun Fight, or others) chosen when a unit is selected to fight.
- **Normal Fight** — fight type for an engaged unit; fights as in Making
  Attacks.
- **Overrun Fight** — fight type for a unit that is (or just became)
  unengaged during the Fight phase; grants one extra pile-in move before
  fighting, and can newly engage previously-unengaged enemy units,
  making them eligible to fight this phase.
- **Emergency disembark move** — the move an embarked unit makes when
  its transport is destroyed (introduced via a worked example; full
  rules live in the Transports section, outside this range).
- **Consolidation move** — a Fight-phase move (up to 3") made in the
  Consolidate step by any unit that was eligible to fight this phase.
- **Consolidation mode** — the mandatory choice (Ongoing, Engaging, or
  Objective Consolidation) governing a consolidation move, assessed in
  that priority order.
- **Ongoing Consolidation** — consolidation mode for an already-engaged
  unit; must stay engaged with every unit it was engaged with.
- **Engaging Consolidation** — consolidation mode when within 3" of
  enemy units (but not engaged); must end engaged with all selected
  enemy units, potentially pulling in units that haven't fought yet.
- **Objective Consolidation** — consolidation mode when within 3" of an
  objective (and no eligible enemy units); must end within range of it.
- **Objective** — a battlefield marker units can move toward/hold
  (defined fully elsewhere in the rulebook).
- **Strategic reserves** — an off-battlefield holding area units can be
  selected to move from/arrive from during the Movement phase (full
  rules in section 20, outside this range).
- **TRANSPORT** — a unit keyword for vehicles that carry embarked units
  (full rules in section 18, outside this range).
