# Core Rules — Basic Rules (pp. 6–25)

Source: *Warhammer 40,000 Core Rules* booklet, "BASIC RULES" section
(01. Core Concepts, 02. Datasheets, 03. Moving, 04. Making Attacks,
05. Attack Sequence, 06. Other Concepts).

---

## 01. Core Concepts

### 01.01 Armies
- Each player commands an **army** made of **units** of **models**.
- A player controls all models in their army.
- **Controlling player**: whichever player controls the models a rule is
  affecting, when a rule refers to "the controlling player."

### 01.02 Units and Models
- A **unit** contains one or more **models**; the models in a unit move and
  fight together as a single group.
- Most models have a **base**, which is treated as part of that model for
  all rules purposes.
- **Friendly** units/models: those in your own army.
- **Enemy** units/models: those in your opponent's army.
- If a rule affects units/models without specifying friendly or enemy, it
  affects *any* unit/model regardless of army.
- If an effect/ability applies to a unit, it applies to **every model** in
  that unit.

### 01.03 Active Player and Opposing Player
- At any time, one player is the **active player**; their opponent is the
  **opposing player**. This designation changes throughout the battle, but
  each player is always one or the other.
- When it is neither player's turn (e.g. start/end of a battle round), the
  player who takes the first turn that battle round is the active player.
- During a player's own turn, that player is normally the active player,
  **except**:
  - Each time a unit is **selected to move**, that unit's controlling
    player is the active player until the move ends.
  - Each time a unit is **selected to shoot** or **selected to fight**,
    that unit's controlling player is the active player until those
    attacks are resolved.
  - (This means during your opponent's turn, if one of your units shoots
    or fights, you briefly become the active player for that action.)

### 01.04 Measuring Distances
- All distances are measured in **inches (")**.
- Players may measure distances at any time (not restricted to a phase or
  turn-order gate).
- When a rule refers to a model's position relative to something else,
  unless otherwise stated, measure to/from **the closest point of that
  model's base**.

### 01.05 Dice
- The game uses six-sided dice (**D6**).
- **2+, 3+, etc.**: a result of 2 or more, 3 or more, etc., satisfies the
  condition.
- **1-3, 4-6, etc.**: any result within the stated range satisfies the
  condition.
- **2D6, 3D6, etc.**: roll that many D6 and add the individual results
  together (e.g. 2D6 = roll two D6 and sum them).
- **D3**: roll one D6 and halve the result, **rounding up**.
- **D6+1, 2D6+3, etc.**: roll the stated dice, sum them, then add the
  stated flat value.

### 01.06 Leadership Rolls
- To make a **leadership roll** for a unit, its controlling player rolls
  **2D6**.
- If the result is **equal to or greater than** any one of the **Ld**
  (Leadership) characteristics in that unit, the roll **succeeds**.
  Otherwise it **fails**.
- The rule that instructed the leadership roll defines the effects of
  success/failure.

### 01.07 Battle-shock Rolls
- To make a **battle-shock roll** for a unit, its controlling player makes
  a **leadership roll** for it.
- If the roll **succeeds**, the unit does not become **battle-shocked**.
- If the roll **fails**, the unit — and every model in it — becomes
  **battle-shocked**.
- While a unit is **battle-shocked**:
  - Its models' **Objective Control (OC)** characteristic is modified to
    **'-'** (i.e. it cannot contribute to controlling objectives).
  - Its controlling player cannot target that unit with **stratagems**.
  - It is **not eligible to start an action**, and any action it had
    already started cannot be completed.
- Battle-shock rolls are most commonly made in the Command phase.

## 02. Datasheets

Every unit has a **datasheet** describing how it functions in battle,
containing the following seven numbered parts:

### 1. Datasheet Name (02.01)
The unit's name.

### 2. Profiles (02.02)
Each model's profile line gives these characteristics:

| Characteristic | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Move (M)** | Speed the model can move, in inches. An `M` of "–" means the model can be set up on the battlefield but can never be moved. |
| **Toughness (T)** | The model's resilience to physical harm; compared against attacking **Strength** on the wound table. |
| **Save (Sv)** | Dice result (e.g. 4+) representing the protection of the model's armour. |
| **Invulnerable Save (InSv)** | Dice result (e.g. 4+) from esoteric protection (force fields, preternatural reflexes, etc.), in addition to physical armour. Not all models have one — if present, it's listed. |
| **Wounds (W)** | How much damage the model can sustain before being **destroyed**. When Wounds are reduced to 0 or fewer, the model is destroyed. |
| **Leadership (Ld)** | Dice result (e.g. 7+) reflecting courage/self-control; used for leadership rolls. |
| **Objective Control (OC)** | How effectively the model controls an **objective**. An `OC` of "–" means the model cannot control objectives at all. |

### 3. Abilities (02.03)
Special rules that may apply during the game, described on the
datasheet.

### 4. Weapons (02.04)
Each weapon profile has these characteristics:

| Characteristic | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Range (R)** | How far a ranged weapon can shoot, in inches. A weapon with `R` of **"Melee"** is a melee weapon. |
| **Attacks (A)** | How many **attack dice** are rolled each time that weapon is used. |
| **Ballistic Skill (BS)** | Dice result (e.g. 4+); accuracy when shooting with that ranged weapon. |
| **Weapon Skill (WS)** | Dice result (e.g. 4+); skill when fighting with that melee weapon. |
| **Strength (S)** | Higher = more likely to wound; compared to target's Toughness on the wound table. |
| **Armour Penetration (AP)** | A modifier (e.g. –1) applied to the target's save roll; larger magnitude = better at defeating armour. |
| **Damage (D)** | The amount of damage inflicted by a successful attack. |

### 5. Keywords (02.05)
- Datasheets list **keywords**, split into **faction keywords** and
  **other keywords**.
- Faction keywords determine which models can be included in an army
  built around a given faction; otherwise both keyword types function
  identically.
- Keywords appear in full capitals, in **KEYWORD BOLD**.
- A rule that applies "to **INFANTRY** units" applies only to units
  carrying that keyword. Singular and plural forms of the same keyword
  function identically (e.g. a rule referencing "INFANTRY" applies
  equally whether the datasheet says "INFANTRY" or a singular variant).

### 6. Unit Composition and Other Rules (02.06)
- Lists the number and types of models in the unit, and each model's
  default **wargear**.
- May list other rules, such as which units a **leader** unit can join,
  or which units can embark within a **TRANSPORT**.

### 7. Wargear Options (02.07)
- Lists options for altering the weapons/other wargear of models in the
  unit when the unit is included in an army.

### Datasheet anatomy example (Boyz, annotated diagram)
The sample datasheet shown (Ork "Boyz") illustrates the 7 numbered zones
above, laid out as:
1. Datasheet name banner (unit name + faction icon area)
2. Profiles table (rows for each distinct model type in the unit, e.g.
   "Boy" and "Boss Nob", columns M/T/Sv/W/Ld/OC)
3. Abilities box (faction ability + any unit-specific abilities, e.g. a
   "Get da Grud Bitz" ability that secures a controlled objective at the
   end of the Command phase)
4. Weapons tables — separate **Ranged Weapons** table (columns: Range, A,
   BS, S, AP, D) and **Melee Weapons** table (columns: Range, A, WS, S,
   AP, D)
5. Keywords box (faction keywords separated from other keywords)
6. Unit Composition box (model count/types and their default wargear
   loadout)
7. Wargear Options box (legal substitutions, e.g. swapping a model's
   default weapon for another)

## 03. Moving

### 03.01 Moving Units
- Several **move types** exist. Each move type defines: which units are
  eligible to make it, its **maximum distance** (or **set-up distance**),
  and any conditions that must be met.
- Named move types referenced elsewhere in the rules (defined in their
  own sections, not detailed on these pages): Normal Move, Advance Move,
  Fall-back Move, Charge Move, Pile-in Move, Consolidation Move, Disembark
  Move, Emergency Disembark Move, Ingress Move, Scout Move, Surge Move.
- Each time you move a unit, you can move one or more of its models, one
  at a time, by moving it in a straight line and/or rotating it, as many
  times as you want (in any combination/order).
- Each time you move a model, unless otherwise stated:
  - It **can** be moved through friendly models.
  - It **can** be moved through any space its base can physically fit
    through.
  - Its base **cannot** be moved through enemy models.
  - Its base **cannot** cross the edge of the battlefield.
  - Any stated **'While Moving'** conditions attached to that move type
    must be met throughout.

#### Moving a Model in a Straight Line
- Move it horizontally across the battlefield.
- Measure from the same point on its base at the start and end of that
  particular straight-line move, and add that distance to any other
  distance the model has already moved since the unit's move began.
- Total distance moved cannot exceed the **maximum distance** of that
  move type.
- Worked example: a model with a 6" maximum distance can move 3" in a
  straight line, then 3" more in a different straight line, for a total
  of 6" moved.

#### Rotating a Model
- Turn it any amount around the centre of its base, keeping it upright.
- Rotating **does not** count toward distance moved.
- A model without a base rotates around its central axis instead.
- Worked example: a model with a 12" maximum distance can move 6" in a
  straight line, rotate, move another 6" in a straight line, rotate again
  — total distance moved is 12" (rotations are free).

#### Ending a Move
After finishing setting up/moving all models in a unit you want to
move/set up, check that **all** of the following apply:
- If the unit is on the battlefield, it is in **coherency** (03.03).
- No model in that unit is on top of, or partway through, the surface of
  a **terrain feature** (e.g. a wall or ceiling).
- All stated **'After Moving'** conditions (attached to that move type)
  are met.

If any of the above conditions are **not** met, that unit and its models
are returned to their positions at the start of that move, and that unit
cannot make that move. Otherwise, after resolving any additional rules
in the move type's 'After Moving' section, that move ends.

### 03.02 Set Up
- Before units can move or attack, they must first be **set up** on the
  battlefield — most commonly during deployment, but this can also occur
  for other reasons during the game.
- Whenever a rule instructs you to set up a unit, place its models on the
  battlefield so that:
  - The unit is in **coherency**.
  - The unit is **unengaged**.
  - All other requirements/restrictions specific to that instruction are
    met.
- **If you cannot set up all models in a unit**, remove that unit from
  the battlefield entirely and return it to its original location (e.g.
  back into **strategic reserves**, or embarked within a **TRANSPORT**).
- Special note (sidebar): if you have to return a unit's models to their
  previous positions because a move couldn't be completed, and it isn't
  possible to set them all back (e.g. the space is no longer free), that
  unit is instead treated as not having been selected to make that move.
  It can be selected to move later, or otherwise attempt to **remain
  stationary** (09.04).

### 03.03 Coherency
- **Coherency** prevents models in the same unit from becoming too
  separated from each other while on the battlefield, ensuring the unit
  sets up and ends every kind of move as a single group.
- A unit containing more than one model must be **set up and end any kind
  of move in coherency**. A unit is in coherency while **both** of the
  following are true for **every** model in that unit:
  - It is within **2" horizontally and 5" vertically** of **at least one
    other model** in that unit.
  - It is within **9" horizontally and 5" vertically** of **every other
    model** in that unit.
- (In effect: models must chain together via 2"/5" links, and the whole
  unit must additionally fit within a 9"/5" envelope of every other
  model.)

#### Regaining Coherency
- In the **End of Turn step** of each player's turn, for any units on the
  battlefield not in coherency, their controlling players must remove
  models from them, one at a time, until they are back in coherency.
- Models removed this way are **destroyed**, but they do **not** trigger
  any rules that apply when a model is destroyed (i.e. no "when this
  model is destroyed" abilities fire).

### 03.04 Engagement
- A model's **engagement range** is the area of the battlefield within
  **2" horizontally and 5" vertically** of it.
- While a friendly model is within engagement range of one or more enemy
  models, those models — and the units they belong to — are **engaged**
  with each other.
- A unit containing no engaged models is **unengaged**.
- (This is the melee-combat proximity zone; per Making Attacks below,
  only engaged units may be targeted by melee weapons.)

## 04. Making Attacks

Each time a unit **shoots** or **fights**, the active player follows
these three steps:

1. **Select Weapons**
2. **Select Targets**
3. **Resolve Attacks**

### 1. Select Weapons (04.01)
- For each model in the attacking unit, select which weapons that model
  will make attacks with.
- Models make **ranged attacks** with ranged weapons, and **melee
  attacks** with melee weapons.
- **While shooting**: you can select one or more ranged weapons that
  model has.
- **While fighting**: you must select exactly **one** melee weapon that
  model has.
- A model with no ranged weapons cannot make ranged attacks; a model with
  no melee weapons cannot make melee attacks.
- Sidearm exception: some models have **[CLOSE-QUARTERS]** weapons in
  addition to other firearms; such models can be an exception to the
  "select any weapons you like" rule, since you may not be able to select
  all of a model's weapons to attack with simultaneously.

### 2. Select Targets (04.02)
For each weapon selected:

**While shooting**: select **one** enemy unit to be the target of that
weapon. Unless otherwise stated, the target must be:
- **Visible** to the model that has that weapon (06.01).
- Within **range** of that weapon.
- **Unengaged**.

**While fighting**: select one or more enemy units to be the targets of
that weapon:
- Each target must be **engaged** with the model that has that weapon.
- You cannot select more targets than that weapon's **A** (Attacks)
  characteristic.

(Sidebar note: if you cannot select a target for a weapon, or choose not
to select one for a ranged weapon, that weapon makes no attacks this
activation. Different weapons on the same unit may be given different
targets.)

### 3. Resolve Attacks (04.03)
Resolve attacks using the following sequence:

1. **Select Enemy Unit**: Select one of the enemy units targeted by one
   or more weapons.
2. **Gather Attack Dice**: Select one weapon (targeting that unit) that
   has not yet been used to make attacks against it, and gather a number
   of D6 equal to that weapon's **A** characteristic — these are **attack
   dice** (each die represents one attack made by an attacking model with
   that weapon). If one or more *other* weapons targeting that same unit
   make **identical attacks** (see below) and haven't yet attacked that
   target, gather their attack dice too, all at once (e.g. three weapons
   making identical attacks, each with A=2, together gather 6 attack
   dice).
3. **Resolve Attack Dice**: Resolve the **attack sequence** (section 05)
   for all attack dice just gathered.
4. **Other Attacks** — follow the first applicable instruction:
   - If other weapons still target the **same** unit and haven't attacked
     yet → return to Gather Attack Dice.
   - Otherwise, if weapons with unresolved attacks target a **different**
     unit → return to Select Enemy Unit.
   - Otherwise, once all weapons have made all their attacks, this
     sequence ends and the attacking unit's attacks are resolved.

#### Identical Attacks
**Identical attacks** are attacks that share the same **BS/WS**, **S**,
**AP**, and **D** characteristics, and which are affected by the same
applicable abilities and rules. Weapons meeting this bar can pool their
attack dice into a single gather/resolve step.

#### Splitting Melee Attacks
- While selecting targets, if you select **more than one** unit as the
  target of a melee weapon, you must **split** that weapon's attacks
  between the target units.
- Declare how many of that weapon's attacks will be made against each
  targeted unit (you must declare **at least one attack per unit
  targeted**).
- In the Gather Attack Dice step, for a weapon splitting its attacks,
  gather only the number of attack dice equal to the number of attacks
  declared against that specific target.

## 05. Attack Sequence

Whenever the active player is instructed to resolve the attack sequence,
they follow these four steps in order. In each step, if there is more
than one die to roll, **all dice for that step are rolled
simultaneously**:

1. **Hit Rolls**
2. **Wound Rolls**
3. **Save Rolls**
4. **Inflict Damage**

This sequence ends for an attack as soon as it fails or inflicts damage.
Once all attacks in the batch have either failed or inflicted damage,
the sequence ends and those attacks are resolved.

### Critical Hits and Critical Wounds
- **Critical hits** are still hits, and **critical wounds** are still
  wounds — they simply also can trigger additional rules keyed off a
  critical hit or critical wound specifically, such as
  **[LETHAL HITS]** and **[DEVASTATING WOUNDS]** (see section 24 keyword
  abilities, outside this range).

### 1. Hit Rolls (05.01)
Make one **hit roll** for each attack die, by rolling one D6. Check each
result against the first matching condition:

| Result | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Unmodified 1 | **Fails** |
| Unmodified 6 | **Critical Hit** |
| Equal to or greater than the attack's **BS/WS** characteristic | **Hit** |
| Any other result | **Fails** |

### 2. Wound Rolls (05.02)
Make one **wound roll** for each hit, by rolling one D6. Check each
result against the first matching condition:

| Result | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Unmodified 1 | **Fails** |
| Unmodified 6 | **Critical Wound** |
| Equal to or greater than the required result (Strength vs Toughness table below) | **Wound** |
| Any other result | **Fails** |

**Strength vs. Toughness table** (required unmodified wound roll, before
the unmodified-1-fails / unmodified-6-critical-wound overrides above):

| Attack's Strength vs Target's Toughness | Required Result |
|---|---|
| Strength is **twice or more than twice** the Toughness | **2+** |
| Strength is **greater than** the Toughness | **3+** |
| Strength is **equal to** the Toughness | **4+** |
| Strength is **less than** the Toughness | **5+** |
| Strength is **half or less than half** the Toughness | **6+** |

### 3. Save Rolls (05.03)
The **opposing player** (defender) resolves this sequence:

1. **Create Groups**: Divide all models in the target unit into groups,
   as many times as required:
   - One group for **each CHARACTER model** (each character gets its own
     group).
   - One group for **all other models that share the same W, Sv, and
     InSv** characteristics (models with identical W/Sv/InSv are grouped
     together).
2. **Allocation Order**: Declare the order in which those groups will
   have attacks allocated to them, applying **all** of the following
   constraints:
   - If a **non-CHARACTER** group contains a model that has already lost
     one or more wounds, that group must be **first** in the allocation
     order.
   - No **CHARACTER** group can be earlier in the allocation order than a
     non-CHARACTER group.
   - **CHARACTER** groups containing a model that has lost one or more
     wounds must be **earlier** in the allocation order than CHARACTER
     groups containing no wounded models.
3. **Make Save Rolls**: The opposing player makes one **save roll** (D6)
   for **each attack that wounded** the target.

### 4. Inflict Damage (05.04)
The opposing player resolves this sequence for each save roll, **working
from lowest result(s) to highest**, until either all attacks are
resolved or all models in the target unit are **destroyed** (in the
latter case, any excess attacks are lost):

1. **Select Model**: Select one model in the **current allocation group**
   (the first/earliest group in the declared order becomes "current";
   once every model in a group is destroyed, the next group in order
   becomes current) — this must be a model that has lost one or more
   wounds **if possible**.
2. **Check Save Roll**: For each result, check the first matching
   condition:

| Condition | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Unmodified 1 | **Inflicts Damage** |
| Model has an **InSv** characteristic, and the (unmodified) result is ≥ that InSv | **Fails** (invulnerable saves ignore AP entirely and are never modified) |
| After modifying the result by the attacking weapon's **AP** characteristic, it is ≥ the **Sv** characteristic of models in the current group | **Fails** |
| Any other result | **Inflicts Damage** |

   - Worked example given: an AP of –1 modifies a save roll of 3 down to
     2. For models with a Sv of 2+ or better, that attack would still
     fail (i.e. the save still succeeds against the attack) since 2 ≥ 2.
3. **Resolve Damage**: If that attack inflicts damage, the selected model
   loses a number of wounds equal to that attack's **D** characteristic.
   If this reduces the model's remaining wounds to 0 or fewer, it is
   **destroyed**.

### Worked Examples (Attack Sequence)

**Example A — Resolving Attack Dice** (2 boltguns [B] + 1 bolt pistol
[BP], BS 3+, S4 vs T3; AP 0, target Sv 3+/InSv 5+):
1. Hit Rolls: 5 attack dice rolled; 4 of 5 hit (BS 3+).
2. Wound Rolls: 4 wound rolls made; S4 vs T3 needs 3+; 3 of 4 wound.
3. Save Rolls: target's controlling player makes 3 save rolls.
4. Inflict Damage: of the three results, the lowest is less than both
   InSv (5+) and Sv (3+), so that attack inflicts damage, destroying the
   model it was allocated to. The next lowest is less than InSv but
   ≥ Sv 3+, so it fails. The remaining result is ≥ InSv 5+, so it also
   fails.

**Example B — Resolving Other Attacks** (1 heavy bolter [HB], BS 4+,
S5 vs T3, AP –1):
1. Hit Rolls: 3 hit rolls made; 2 of 3 hit.
2. Wound Rolls: 2 wound rolls made; S5 vs T3 needs 3+; both wound.
3. Save Rolls: 2 save rolls made.
4. Inflict Damage: the lowest result, modified by AP –1, is less than the
   target's Sv 3+, so it inflicts damage, destroying the model. The other
   result is ≥ the target's InSv 5+, so it fails.

**Example C — Attacking Attached Units** (illustrates targeting an
**attached unit** — one formed from a bodyguard unit plus one or more
attached **CHARACTER** models, e.g. a unit with a Saint Celestine-type
character and Geminae Superia attached to a Seraphim unit):
- Attacker selects weapons: 7 boltguns (B), 1 plasma pistol (PP), 2 heavy
  bolters (HB).
- All selected weapons are in range of, and the target is visible to, the
  attached unit as a single target.
- Attack dice are gathered: the heavy bolters are resolved separately
  (3 attacks each = 6 dice total, not identical to the boltguns); the
  boltguns (7 × A2 = 14 dice) and plasma pistol (1 × A1 = 1 die) are
  gathered together/separately per their own identical-attacks groupings.

**Example D — Allocation Groups** (continuing the attached-unit example):
1. Create Groups and Declare Order: target's controlling player splits
   the attached unit into three groups — one for the character
   (Celestine-type model), one for the Geminae Superia, one for the
   Seraphim. They declare the allocation order as Geminae Superia first
   (banking on their Sv/InSv), then Seraphim, then the character last
   (a CHARACTER group must not precede a non-CHARACTER group, and an
   unwounded CHARACTER group must come after any wounded CHARACTER
   group).
2. Resolve Attack Dice: the heavy bolters wound the target 5 times, so 5
   save rolls are made.
3. Select Next Group: attacks are resolved one at a time, from lowest
   save roll to highest. The two results of 1 are allocated first to the
   current group (Geminae Superia) and destroy both Geminae Superia
   models (unmodified 1 always inflicts damage regardless of Sv/InSv).
   The next result of 3, modified by the attacking weapon's AP of –1,
   also inflicts damage, destroying one Seraphim model (now the current
   group, since the Geminae Superia group is fully destroyed). The
   remaining attacks fail, so no further damage is inflicted.

## 06. Other Concepts

### 06.01 Visibility
- **Line of sight** is used to determine visibility between models.
- For an observing model to have line of sight to another model, it must
  be possible to draw an imaginary straight line, **1mm wide**, from any
  part of the observing model to any part of the observed model. This
  imaginary line is the **line of sight**.
- While checking line of sight, other models — both in the observing
  model's own unit and in the observed model's unit — are **ignored**
  (they do not block the line).
- Other models/units can be **visible** or **fully visible** to the
  observing model:
  - **Model visible**: if *any part* of another model is visible to the
    observing model, that model is **visible**.
  - **Model fully visible**: if *every part* of another model that is
    facing the observing model is visible to it (i.e. the only thing
    blocking visibility to any part of that other model is that model
    itself), that model is **fully visible**.
  - **Unit visible**: if one or more models in a unit are visible to the
    observing model, that unit is **visible**.
  - **Unit fully visible**: if every model in a unit is fully visible to
    the observing model, that unit is **fully visible**. When determining
    this, the observing model can see through other models in that same
    target unit.
- Terrain applies additional rules to visibility (covered in the Terrain
  section, outside this page range — reference only).

### 06.02 Mortal Wounds
- Some attacks/rules inflict **mortal wounds** on units directly (bypassing
  hit/wound/save rolls).
- Each time a unit suffers one or more mortal wounds, its controlling
  player resolves the following sequence for each mortal wound, until
  either all of them have been inflicted or the unit is **destroyed**:
  1. **Select Model** — following the first applicable instruction:
     - If a non-CHARACTER model in the unit has lost one or more wounds,
       you must select that model.
     - Otherwise, if the unit contains one or more non-CHARACTER models,
       you must select one of those models.
     - Otherwise, if one or more CHARACTER models in the unit have lost
       one or more wounds, you must select one of those models.
     - Otherwise, you must select one CHARACTER model in the unit.
  2. **Resolve Damage** — the selected model loses 1 wound. If this
     reduces its remaining wounds to 0, it is **destroyed**.
- **Mortal wounds and normal damage**: when resolving attack dice that
  inflict a mixture of both mortal wounds and normal damage, resolve
  **all normal damage first**, then resolve **all the mortal wounds**.

### 06.03 Hazard Rolls
- To make a **hazard roll** for a unit, roll one D6:
  - On a **1–2**, the roll **fails** and that unit suffers **1 mortal
    wound** — or **3 mortal wounds** instead if **every model** in that
    unit is a **MONSTER/VEHICLE** model.
- If more than one hazard roll is required for a unit, make **all** of
  those rolls **simultaneously**.

## Key defined terms

- **Army** — all units controlled by one player in a game.
- **Controlling player** — the player who controls the unit/model a rule
  refers to.
- **Unit** — one or more models that move and fight together as a group.
- **Model** — an individual miniature; its base counts as part of it.
- **Friendly unit/model** — one in your own army.
- **Enemy unit/model** — one in your opponent's army.
- **Active player** — the player currently taking actions/whose units are
  acting; can shift mid-turn when the opponent's units shoot or fight.
- **Opposing player** — whichever player is not the active player at a
  given moment.
- **Line of sight** — the imaginary 1mm-wide straight line used to check
  visibility between models.
- **Visible / fully visible** (model or unit) — degrees of line-of-sight
  visibility; see 06.01 definitions above.
- **Leadership roll** — 2D6 rolled for a unit, succeeding if ≥ any one
  Ld characteristic in that unit.
- **Battle-shock roll** — a leadership roll made specifically to
  determine whether a unit becomes battle-shocked.
- **Battle-shocked** — a state reducing a unit's OC to '-', preventing it
  being targeted by stratagems, and blocking action eligibility/
  completion.
- **Objective Control (OC)** — a model characteristic governing its
  ability to contribute to controlling objectives; '-' means none.
- **Datasheet** — the full rules card for a unit (name, profiles,
  abilities, weapons, keywords, unit composition, wargear options).
- **Move (M)** — a model's maximum normal movement speed characteristic.
- **Toughness (T)** — resilience to harm; used vs Strength on the wound
  table.
- **Save (Sv)** — armour save dice-result characteristic.
- **Invulnerable Save (InSv)** — a save from non-armour sources, ignoring
  AP, never modified.
- **Wounds (W)** — damage capacity before destruction.
- **Destroyed** — a model removed from play after Wounds reach 0 or fewer.
- **Leadership (Ld)** — dice-result characteristic used in leadership
  rolls.
- **Range (R)** — a weapon's maximum shooting distance, or "Melee".
- **Attacks (A)** — number of attack dice a weapon generates per use.
- **Ballistic Skill (BS)** — ranged accuracy dice-result characteristic.
- **Weapon Skill (WS)** — melee accuracy dice-result characteristic.
- **Strength (S)** — a weapon's wounding power, compared to Toughness.
- **Armour Penetration (AP)** — modifier applied against the target's
  save roll.
- **Damage (D)** — wounds lost by the target model per successful attack.
- **Keyword** — a capitalized tag on a datasheet (faction keyword or
  other keyword) that rules can reference.
- **Faction keyword** — a keyword used to determine army-building
  legality; otherwise functions like any other keyword.
- **Leader** — a unit type that can join certain other units (bodyguard
  units), per datasheet rules.
- **Transport** — a unit type that other units can embark within.
- **Wargear options** — datasheet-listed legal substitutions for a unit's
  default weapons/equipment.
- **Move type** — a named category of movement (Normal, Advance, Fall
  Back, Charge, Pile-in, Consolidation, Disembark, Emergency Disembark,
  Ingress, Scout, Surge) each with its own max/set-up distance and
  conditions.
- **Maximum distance** — the movement cap for a given move type.
- **Set-up distance** — the placement-distance cap for a move type used
  to set up units (rather than move already-deployed ones).
- **Coherency** — the requirement that every model in a multi-model unit
  be within 2"/5" of at least one other unit member and within 9"/5" of
  every other unit member, at the end of any move or set-up.
- **Engagement range** — the 2" horizontal / 5" vertical zone around a
  model within which enemy models become engaged with it.
- **Engaged** — the state of models/units within each other's engagement
  ranges.
- **Unengaged** — a unit containing no engaged models.
- **Terrain feature** — a piece of scenery a model's base cannot end atop
  or partway through the surface of after moving.
- **Select weapons / select targets / resolve attacks** — the three
  top-level steps of making an attack.
- **[CLOSE-QUARTERS]** — a keyword marking sidearm-type ranged weapons
  with special selection interactions during Select Weapons.
- **Identical attacks** — attacks sharing BS/WS, S, AP, and D
  characteristics and affected by the same abilities/rules, allowing
  their attack dice to be pooled.
- **Attack dice** — the D6 gathered (one per Attacks characteristic
  point) to resolve a batch of attacks.
- **Splitting attacks** — dividing a single melee weapon's attacks among
  multiple engaged target units, with at least one attack declared per
  target.
- **Hit roll** — a D6 rolled per attack die to determine whether it hits.
- **Critical hit** — an unmodified 6 on a hit roll.
- **Wound roll** — a D6 rolled per hit to determine whether it wounds,
  per the Strength-vs-Toughness table.
- **Critical wound** — an unmodified 6 on a wound roll.
- **[LETHAL HITS]** / **[DEVASTATING WOUNDS]** — keyword abilities (fully
  defined elsewhere, section 24) triggered off critical hits/wounds
  respectively.
- **Save roll** — a D6 rolled per wound to determine whether the target's
  armour/invulnerable save prevents damage.
- **Allocation group** — a subdivision of a target unit (one per
  CHARACTER model, plus one for all other models sharing W/Sv/InSv) used
  to order how wounding attacks are allocated.
- **Current allocation group** — the first group in the allocation order
  that still contains un-destroyed models; attacks are allocated to it
  until it is exhausted, then the next group becomes current.
- **Character (CHARACTER)** — a keyword marking models subject to special
  allocation-order rules (must go last relative to non-CHARACTER groups,
  wounded before unwounded).
- **Attached unit** — a bodyguard unit with one or more CHARACTER models
  attached to it, treated as a single target/allocation unit.
- **Mortal wound** — damage inflicted directly on a unit, bypassing hit/
  wound/save rolls, resolved as 1 lost wound per mortal wound via the
  Select Model procedure.
- **Hazard roll** — a D6 rolled for a unit that on a 1-2 inflicts 1 mortal
  wound (3 if every model in the unit is MONSTER/VEHICLE).
- **Monster/Vehicle** — keyword(s) referenced by the hazard roll's
  increased-mortal-wounds condition.
